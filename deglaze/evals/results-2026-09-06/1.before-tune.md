Verdict: Needs surgery — this is three products in a trench coat, and only one of them was invited.

Not wrong about: Daily channel summaries are a real, repeatedly validated want, especially for people returning from time off or watching too many channels.

What's wobbling:
1. Auto-assigning action items from chat is the feature that gets the bot muted. "auto-assigns action items to people" turns casual sentences into obligations without consent. One wrong assignment to a director and the whole thing loses trust. Nothing in the pitch shows anyone asked for assignment, only that summaries exist.
2. "Teams will love it" is an assertion doing unpaid overtime. No team named, no size, no channel count, no quote from a user, no mention of what they use today.
3. "Tracks them in Jira, and sends nudges when they slip" stacks Jira write access, a Slack app with broad read scope, and unsolicited DMs. Each one is an admin approval cycle. Assumption: you have not yet cleared any of them.

Cut first: Assignment and nudges. Ship summaries only, and make anything that creates a Jira ticket require one human click.

Prove me wrong: Hand-write a daily summary for one busy channel for five workdays. Count replies, reactions, and anyone asking for it on day six. If nobody notices when you stop, the rest of the roadmap doesn't matter.

Confidence: medium — one sentence of pitch, no evidence about the target team, their channel volume, or whether Jira is already where their work lives.
