Verdict: Worth a cheap test — three symptoms, one diagnosis, and nobody checked whether the patient is the survey or the funnel.

Not wrong about: If the answers change nothing downstream, the survey is pure friction, and deleting friction nobody consumes is the correct default.

What's wobbling:
1. "Completion is 11%" is doing double duty. It reads as proof the survey is bad, but the target never says whether the 89% who bail also bail on onboarding entirely. If they skip the survey and keep going, the survey is annoying. If they leave the product, the survey may be sitting on top of a deeper drop and removal will not fix the number you care about.
2. "Do not change anything downstream" is asserted, not shown. Nothing in the target names who was asked. Sales, success, and marketing routinely read survey exports out of band, in spreadsheets nobody in product knows about. Assumption: no one audited consumers before declaring the data dead.
3. Removing the survey removes the only self-reported segmentation you have. The pitch never says what replaces it. Six months from now someone asks "which users are agencies" and the answer is a shrug.

Cut first: The word "removing." Make it "hiding behind a flag" so the rollback is a toggle instead of a rebuild.

Prove me wrong: Flag the survey off for 50% of new signups for two weeks. Compare onboarding completion and day-7 activation between arms. If the no-survey arm wins or ties, delete it for real. Separately, email every team lead one question: "Do you use the onboarding survey data anywhere?" Wait 48 hours.

Confidence: medium — I have three summary claims and no funnel, no ticket count, and no list of who reads the data today.
