Verdict: Worth a cheap test — three symptoms, one autopsy, and the corpse hasn't been examined.

Not wrong about: If the answers change nothing downstream, the survey is a tax with no receipt. That alone justifies action.

What's wobbling:
1. "The answers do not change anything downstream" is asserted, not shown. Nothing in the pitch names who checked. Personalization, sales routing, and analytics segments are the usual quiet consumers, and they don't file tickets when the feed goes dry.
2. Eleven percent completion could mean the survey is bad, or it could mean the survey is in the wrong place, too long, or optional and skippable. The pitch treats one number as a diagnosis. Removal is the right fix for only one of those causes.
3. Assumption: nothing measures what removal does to activation. If the survey is a speed bump that filters out low-intent signups, day-7 numbers drop and nobody connects it back to this decision.

Cut first: The word "removing." Replace it with "disabling behind a flag."

Prove me wrong: Grep for the survey's field names in analytics, CRM sync, and any conditional UI. Then flag it off for half of new signups for two weeks and compare day-7 activation.

Confidence: medium — I have three summary claims and no data on who consumes the answers or what the tickets actually say.
