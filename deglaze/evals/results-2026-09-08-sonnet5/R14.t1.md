Verdict: Needs surgery — you're picking a cadence and an audience before you've picked a goal.

Trying to do: Start a recurring email to every customer summarizing what shipped.

Keep: forcing a written record of what shipped on a regular cadence is better discipline than sporadic ad-hoc announcements, and it's cheap to prototype.

Change:
1. "All customers" skips segmentation. Different customers use different features and plans; a changelog that's 80% irrelevant to a given reader reads as noise, not signal, and noise is what drives unsubscribes and spam complaints. The question never asks who this is actually for.
2. No stated goal. Fewer support tickets, higher feature adoption, retention, upsell — each implies different content, tone, and success metric. "Monthly changelog" is a format in search of a purpose; without the goal you can't tell if monthly is even the right cadence or if this is worth the ongoing effort at all.
3. Consent and email classification are unaddressed. Sending to "all customers" blurs transactional vs. marketing email — those have different consent and unsubscribe obligations depending on jurisdiction and what's already in your list's opt-in status. Assuming you can blanket-email your whole customer base monthly by default is a compliance risk, not a detail to sort out later.

Risks: monthly may not match your actual shipping rate — some months could be thin enough to force filler, others too dense for a digest. Also unaddressed: who owns writing and reviewing this every month, which is a recurring cost, not a one-time launch.

Prove me wrong: pull the last 6 months of shipped changes and draft what one email per month would have actually contained. If half the months have nothing customer-relevant, monthly is the wrong cadence before you've sent one.

Confidence: high — plain judgment call, no artifact or code to verify, zero tool calls used.
