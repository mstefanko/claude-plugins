Verdict: Needs surgery — the pitch skips the one mechanism the entire idea depends on.

Trying to do: Validate and justify building an AI app that plans a week of dinners from fridge contents for busy parents.

Keep: The insight is real — "what's for dinner" is a genuinely loathed daily decision, and using what's already on hand instead of a shopping list is the right lever to pull.

Change:
1. How the app knows what's in the fridge is never specified. Manual entry defeats "for busy parents" on day one; computer vision/inventory tracking is a hard, expensive problem multiple funded products (Innit, early Whisk/SuperCook attempts) have struggled with for years. This isn't a detail to fill in later — it's the mechanism the whole pitch rests on, and right now it's a blank.
2. "Every parent I have talked to says they would use it" is stated intent, not behavior. Saying yes to a friend pitching an idea costs nothing; paying for it, or doing the fridge-input work daily, costs something. Nothing here distinguishes enthusiasm from commitment.
3. "I know this will work" has no definition of "work" attached — installs, week-two retention, willingness to pay are three different bars, and the claim doesn't say which one it's clearing or how.

Risks: Several dinner-planning apps have already hit the input-friction wall and stalled — plausible but I haven't checked current market state, so treat as unconfirmed.

Prove me wrong: Get 20 of those parents to manually log fridge contents daily for one week (no app, just a form) and measure how many finish day 7.

Confidence: high — pitch was pasted in full, zero tool calls, nothing here rests on a checkable repo claim.
