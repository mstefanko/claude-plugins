Change:
1. "Obviously" is doing the argument's job. The claim jumps from "customers prefer it" straight to "we're doing it," with nothing showing usage-based pricing holds or grows revenue versus per-seat, for these accounts or the base as a whole.
2. Top 10 customers are the worst sample for this call. They're the accounts most likely to carry either high seat counts (making per-seat expensive for them) or steady heavy usage (making usage-based cheap for them) — exactly the profile that would say "prefer" regardless of what it does to company revenue. Preference from the people who benefit from a smaller bill isn't evidence the bill should shrink.
3. "Prefer" was asked, not tested. A stated hypothetical isn't behavior under a live meter. There's no pilot and no back-test of what these 10 accounts would actually have paid last year under usage-based terms versus their real per-seat spend.

Risks: The rest of the customer base — smaller accounts with low seats but bursty usage — could see costs rise under usage-based pricing in a way the top 10 never would. A revenue simulation across the full book, not just the top 10, would settle it.

Prove me wrong: Take the top 10 accounts' actual usage logs from the past 12 months and compute what they'd have paid under usage-based terms versus what they actually paid per-seat.
