Trying to do: Justify entering the dog-walking marketplace business by sizing the opportunity off Uber's valuation.

Keep: The underlying insight — pet care has recurring, schedulable, trust-sensitive service demand that supports a marketplace — is a real category, not an invented one.

Change:
1. $60B is Uber's valuation, not a market size. A valuation is investors pricing future cash flows across rides, Eats, freight, and years of growth — it isn't a dollar figure of "the ride-hailing market" you can carve a percentage from. "Even 1% of that" mixes units that don't match; there's no market-size number here to take 1% of, so the "huge" conclusion isn't computed, it's asserted.
2. The comparison skips that this has already been run. Rover and Wag are literally "marketplace for dog walking" businesses that reached public markets, and neither came close to Uber-scale outcomes — Wag's SPAC-era valuation collapsed, Rover was taken private at a fraction of its debut price. The pitch treats "Uber for dog walking" as an unproven model when it's already been tried twice with underwhelming results, and says nothing about why this attempt would diverge from those two.

Risks: Dog walking may lack the density and per-transaction economics that made ride-hailing's network effects work at Uber's scale — unconfirmed, but comparing against Rover/Wag's actual unit economics would settle it.

Prove me wrong: Show Rover + Wag's combined current revenue or GMV, and show that 1% of Uber's valuation is a meaningful multiple of it.
