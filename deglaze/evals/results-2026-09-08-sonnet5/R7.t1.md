Change:
1. No stuck-job recovery. The design describes backoff and dead-letter for jobs that fail inside processing, but nothing reclaims a job whose worker crashes or gets killed after claiming it. Once the claim transaction commits, the row sits in whatever "in progress" state with no lease, heartbeat, or timeout to move it back to ready. A crashed worker means that job is silently gone forever — worse than the Redis queue it's replacing, unless something you haven't described is watching for this.
2. Transaction scope is unstated and consequential. "Workers claim in batches of 50 inside a transaction" doesn't say whether the transaction closes right after claiming or stays open through job execution. If it's the latter, any slow job holds an open transaction that blocks autovacuum from advancing across the whole jobs table, not just those 50 rows — the classic way this exact pattern rots under load. The 40ms p99 was measured at launch, not after days of that bloat.

Risks: No stated retention or archival for completed/dead-lettered rows at ~2.9M jobs/day; table growth could erode the very index this design leans on. Checking row count and index size after a week of real traffic would settle it.
