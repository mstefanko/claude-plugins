Verdict: Needs surgery — "unlimited" and "cheap" are doing a lot of unpaid overtime in that sentence.

Trying to do: Use a free, uncapped PDF-to-text tier as a signup funnel, betting compute cost stays low and at least 5% of those signups convert to paid.

Keep: Tying the free hook to the actual core feature (not some unrelated freebie) is the right instinct — free users self-select as people who need exactly the paid product.

Change:
1. "Compute is cheap" is asserted, not shown, and "unlimited" is the word that breaks it. Text-layer extraction is cheap; OCR on scanned/image PDFs is not, and an unlimited tier has no mechanism to keep users on the cheap path. The claim needs a per-page cost number and a worst-case mix, not an adjective.
2. Unlimited with no cap or rate limit turns the free tier into a hosted OCR service for anyone who scripts against it — no account limit, no throttle, nothing in the pitch that distinguishes a real prospective customer from a bot hitting the endpoint in a loop. That's a cost-control invariant with no guard on it, not a hypothetical.

A 5% conversion number is stated with no comparable, no pilot, and no account of what upgrade trigger exists when the free tier already does everything. Unlimited free and a confident conversion rate are in tension, and the pitch resolves that tension by not mentioning it.

Prove me wrong: Cap the free tier at a concrete page/file limit, price out compute for that cap under a realistic scanned-PDF mix, and check whether the margin still clears at a 5% paid conversion rate.
