Trying to do: Decide whether to roll a new onboarding flow out to 100% of users based on one A/B test.

Keep: Running a controlled experiment before flipping the switch for everyone, instead of shipping on vibes, is the right instinct.

Change:
1. p=0.09 is being reported as "lifted retention 12%" — a causal claim. At the conventional 0.05 bar this result isn't significant; a result this size has roughly a 1-in-11 chance of showing up with no real effect at all. The rollout plan treats "maybe" as "yes" and is betting a full launch on it.
2. The test ran one week, but day-7 retention needs seven full days of observation per user. Anyone enrolled in the back half of the week hasn't had the chance to hit day 7 yet — meaning either those users got dropped (shrinking n=180 further) or their status got counted early/wrong. The metric doesn't fit inside the window it was measured in, which alone could produce a spurious "lift."
3. "Rolling out to everyone Monday" has no monitoring or rollback step attached. Two weak test arms of 180 don't tell you what happens at full traffic; nothing here says what gets watched after launch or what triggers a revert if it goes sideways.

Risks: n=180/arm may simply be underpowered for a 12% effect — the p=0.09 is consistent with "too few users to tell," not just "borderline real." A power calculation using the observed variance would settle whether 180/arm could ever clear significance for an effect this size.

Prove me wrong: Rerun the same variants for one more full week with a matured cohort (every user has a real day-7 outcome) and check if p drops below 0.05.
