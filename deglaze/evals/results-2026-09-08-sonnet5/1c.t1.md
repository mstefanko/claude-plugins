Verdict: Needs surgery — this is three products in a trench coat.

Trying to do: Turn chat into tracked, nudged work without anyone deciding to opt into that.

Keep: Daily channel summaries is a real, bounded, low-risk feature on its own — worth shipping and testing before anything else.

Change:
1. Auto-assignment is asserted, not asked for. Nothing in the pitch shows anyone wanting a bot to decide who owns what — that's the exact behavior that gets a bot muted or banned from a channel, because it assigns work without the assignee agreeing to it.
2. "Teams will love it" is the whole validation. No pilot, no quote, no channel count, no comparison to what people do today (manual EOD summaries, existing Jira triage). The certainty in "I am certain this is the right call" is carrying the entire case by itself.
3. Jira write access plus automated nudges is a scope jump the pitch treats as free. Writing tickets into someone else's tracker and pestering them about deadlines the bot itself set needs an approval and rollback path (who can undo a wrong auto-assignment, what happens when the bot mis-parses a thread) — none of which exists in the pitch.

Risks: Summarization alone may hallucinate or misattribute action items from ambiguous threads, creating tickets nobody agreed to. A week of summaries with humans manually converting to tickets would show the error rate before automating the write path.

Prove me wrong: Run the summarizer alone in one channel for a week, no assignment, no Jira writes. Count how many summaries actually get read, and how many "action items" a human would have created differently than the bot did.

Confidence: high — target was fully in the message, zero tool calls.
